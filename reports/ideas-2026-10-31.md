# MojiDiff: ideas at the wrap-up

2026-10-31, collected from the brainstorms after the wrap-up. Each idea says what it is, why it fits the evidence
(`reports/learnings-2026-10-31.md`) and what it would cost. Where an idea was tried, the status line says so and
names the finding.

The shared premise: the transcriber copies shapes it has memorised. With 2,681 training icons the bottleneck is
data and drawing ability, not compute. A learned model is only justified by a job a tracer cannot do. vtracer is 99%
recognisable at 1.6 ms on a CPU.

## A real-time art installation (no cluster needed)

For art, the model's weakness becomes a feature. It snaps whatever it sees to the emoji it remembers, and when
lost it produces fragments that read as glitch art. Its strongest families (faces, hands, busts, stick figures)
are exactly what face and hand tracking produce.

**Speed budget.** One icon takes about 0.4 s on the RTX 4080 and about 1.2 s in the browser (WASM). That is not
30 fps per icon, but every decoder step adds a token and every few tokens complete a path. The drawing can stream
path by path, with a new SVG every 1-2 s.

1. **Emoji mirror.** MediaPipe face and hand landmarks from a webcam, in the browser, are drawn as black
   OpenMoji-style lines into the 144 px frame, as the drawing pad in `web/demo` already does. The model draws an
   emoji of the visitor. A raised hand gives the raised-hand emoji, one of its most faithful icons, and partial
   matches give half-broken emoji. Only the landmark-to-lines step is new.
2. **Watch it draw.** The autoregressive drawing is the artwork: each path is shown as the model commits to it,
   with OpenMoji's palette filling in. At higher temperature it becomes an endless, never-repeating drawing
   machine. It needs a per-step callback in `decode.mjs` that returns the partial program (1-2 hours).
3. **Dream loop.** The model's SVG is rendered, slightly noised and fed back as the next input. The image drifts
   into the model's attractors, the emoji it knows best, until the audience (a hand movement or a sound) pushes it
   out again. No training; an evening of code.
4. **Air drawing.** A tracked index finger is a pen in the air. Every half second the stroke so far goes to the
   model, and the gesture snaps into emoji parts and colours. The near misses are the point. The doodle
   fine-tunes (`reports/doodle/2026-10-31-wave-d1/`) failed as converters but would serve here.
5. **Latent drift.** A slow walk through the canvas latent space with the flow prior (checkpoints vt-v3-d02-s5 and lfp-v2x),
   decoded continuously. Faces morph into faces and dissolve into fragments outside the dense regions, steered by
   body position or sound. It runs in Python on a GPU (about 0.8 s per frame on the 4080); the browser would need
   a new ONNX export.

**Suggested build.** Concepts 1 and 2 together as the main piece, the emoji mirror drawing live in the browser on
the 4080 machine, with concept 3 as the idle screen. Prototype about 3-4 hours on top of `web/demo`. Expect a
slowly morphing piece at a few frames per second, not frame-accurate tracking.

## Uses for large compute (a B300 cluster)

Precomputation can make an installation feel real-time where the models are not.

1. **Generated emoji atlas.** Sample hundreds of thousands of new emoji from the flow prior, canvas latent and
   transcriber at various temperatures, render them, drop fragments and copies of training icons, and index by
   CLIP embedding. The installation answers any pose, sketch or face in milliseconds with the nearest generated
   emoji, and the atlas is an artwork in itself. Needs only a sampling script.
2. **Morph library.** Precompute smooth 24-60-frame morphs between a few hundred popular emoji. Generate several
   candidates per frame and keep the smoothest path, so the installation blends fluidly instead of waiting about a
   second per frame.
3. **Map of the model's memory.** Run the dream loop from tens of thousands of starting points and record where
   each trajectory ends: the model's basins of attraction, as a map or animation and as a picture of what it
   learned.
4. **Tiny-model sweep.** Train 8 small variants side by side (fewer or narrower decoder layers, distilled from the
   current model's outputs) and plot milliseconds per icon against visual grade. The goal is a full emoji in under
   100 ms in the browser, where the current model takes 1.2 s. This is the one idea that moves the original
   deliverable.
5. **Render-reward RL on the small model.** *Tried 2026-10-31 18:30-19:55 (findings, "Render-reward RL on the
   transcriber").* The operator stopped it at steps 250-300, inside the supervised controls' range. The method
   is in `render2svg.render_reward_loss`, tested. Open: more steps, larger groups, or a reward on unseen shapes.

## Model-side bets to close open questions

1. **Text-to-emoji with a pretrained image model plus a tracer.** A style LoRA (SDXL or similar) on OpenMoji's
   ~4,000 renders, with vtracer vectorising the output. It is the most reliable route to new emoji in OpenMoji
   style, and it gives up "small and from scratch". The SVGs are traced fills, not OpenMoji's strokes.
   About 1 h setup and 1-2 h training on a few GPUs.
2. **Pre-training on random synthetic programs.** Millions of random SVG programs in our codec (random paths,
   strokes, palette fills) teach the transcriber to trace any shape before OpenMoji fine-tuning. It attacks the
   core failure, copying memorised shapes. Test: do vampires and fairies stop becoming plain people? Odds roughly
   even; about 1.5 h building and 2-3 h training.
3. **Data-scaling curve.** The current recipe on 25%, 50% and 100% of the training icons, two seeds each,
   extrapolated to "recognisable like vtracer". It turns "the limit is the data" into a number. No new code;
   6-8 GPUs for about an hour.
4. **Best-of-N ceiling.** 64 or 256 candidates per icon instead of 8, keeping the best render match. Best of 8
   was the one visible gain (52% to 65% recognisable); this shows how far sampling alone goes.
5. **Frozen pretrained vision encoder.** DINOv2 or CLIP in front of our decoder, training only the decoder and
   adapter. It separates "cannot see the shape" from "cannot draw it". Expected gain small: the failure looks
   like drawing.
6. **Best of 8 in the browser.** The one model-side gain that shows by eye, on WebGPU or as background work
   (see the open demo questions in `state/CURRENT.md`).

## Ruled out by evidence

- **Standard fine-tuning of OmniSVG to OpenMoji.** Tried five ways in September (Gates M and N). With teacher
  forcing it was 90-100% exact; drawing on its own, it misplaced the first point (median 56 of 200 units) and
  looped. A sequence-level objective (render-reward RL on OmniSVG) was named as the remedy and never run;
  realistically 2-3 h of building, about one in three to work. The untouched model with best of 6 put the right
  icon first for 77% of drawings.
- **OmniLottie as an animator** (findings 2026-10-31, `reports/omnilottie/2026-10-31-zero-shot/`). Zero-shot, it
  made 15 of 30 icons recognisable from the image, none faithful, with almost no motion, at 64.5 s per icon on a
  B300. Fine-tuning would need animated OpenMoji-style data, which does not exist.
- **More recipe arms on the transcriber.** Dropout 0.2 was the one lever; everything else is level at 2,681 icons.
